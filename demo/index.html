    How this demo works

    Everything runs in your browser: a JavaScript prototype of the stomp draft spec tokenizes, parses (following grammar.peg), resolves templates and inheritance, generates pads, solves every relational placement and draws each layer — on every keystroke.

    Using it

    • Type on the left; the footprint redraws a moment after you pause. Errors are underlined and listed under the code.
    • Put the cursor on a statement to highlight the objects it creates or moves. Placements draw their dimension right on the footprint, so pad 3 is 4.56 right of pad 1 shows the 4.56 between the two centres.
    • Hover a pad for its size, position, mask and paste; its source lines light up. Click it to jump to the statement that defines it.
    • Drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom, double-click to fit. The preview follows the footprint your cursor is in.
    • 3D: switch the preview to 3D to see the part on a board. The body, the pins and the lead form already describe it: lead gullwing … bends gull-wing leads down to the span, lead ball puts balls under a substrate, lead endcap caps a chip. A model { … } block adjusts standoff, height, draft, colors and the pin-1 mark, or adds boxes and cylinders; lead 4 gullwing 3.0 x 0.9 gives one pin its own lead (and land). Export the model as VRML for KiCad, or STL.
    • Export to KiCad saves every footprint in the file as a KiCad 9 library (a zipped .pretty folder). Each footprint carries its 3D model inside it, so the library needs no model folder.
    • Libraries: a template can name the footprints it makes (name "QFN-{n}-…"), and footprints from qfn { … } makes one per table row. Library shows every footprint in the file; put the cursor on a row to see that one. Open the QFN library example to try it.
    • In the editor: Tab indents, Ctrl/⌘+/ toggles comments, Esc then Tab leaves the editor.

    Choices the draft leaves open

    Where the spec doesn't settle a meaning, the demo picks one:

    • Generated pads are sized across × along. For pins … generators, W x L is width across the row by length along the lead, and each side's pads are rotated to face outward. That is how default pad rect 0.6 x 2.2 reads in the SOIC hand-solder variant.
    • Floating layouts are anchored by the origin rule. A purely relational footprint (TO-252) has nothing absolute to start from, so it is solved relative to its first object and then centred on the body.
    • One coordinate frame. at (x, y) and graphic coordinates share one frame; origin re-bases the finished footprint.
    • IPC-style lands use IPC-7351B fillet goals with nominal part dimensions (no part tolerances), F = 0.05 mm, P = 0.025 mm, rounded corners at 25%. Ball lands are 80% of the ball. Mask expansion defaults to 0.05 mm per side.
    • Manual courtyards replace the automatic one. A courtyard { … } block replaces the auto outline, which is the only way a polygon courtyard makes sense.
    • Interpolation prints numbers in their shortest form. Lengths print in the file's units, and {e:.2} fixes the decimals.
    • The body's height is the seated height (JEDEC A): the top of the part above the board. The body sits on its standoff (A1), which each lead form defaults: 0.1 mm for gull-wings, 0.02 for leadless parts, three quarters of the ball for a BGA.
    • Small grammar extensions. omit/only accept name ranges (only 1..5, as the syntax zoo uses), and sizes may be written without spaces (0.6x1.5).

    Not in this demo

    step files aren't loaded: a footprint that names one shows its generated stand-in, and its KiCad export refers to the file instead of embedding a model. Castellated leads draw a provisional land, pending SPEC §17.7. Export is to KiCad 9 and later only.

    Layers

    Copper
    pads, with drills and thermal vias
    Paste
    stencil openings, including windowpanes
    Mask
    solder-mask openings (off by default)
    Silk
    auto outline pulled back from copper, pin-1 and polarity marks
    Courtyard
    hull of land and body plus IPC clearance
    Fab
    assembly outline and reference
    Constraints
    dimensions from placement statements
