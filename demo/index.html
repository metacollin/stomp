    How this demo works

    Everything runs in your browser. On every keystroke, a JavaScript version of the stomp draft spec parses the file (following grammar.peg), expands templates and inheritance, generates pads, solves the placements and draws each layer.

    Using it

    • Type on the left. The footprint redraws when you pause. Errors are underlined and listed under the code.
    • The syntax: a block is a header line, its statements, then end. A named value takes a colon (pitch: 1.27), and w = 1.2 gives a name a value. A keyword counts only where the grammar expects it, so a name can be any word but x and end.
    • Put the cursor on a statement to highlight what it creates or moves. Placements draw their dimension on the footprint: pad 3 is 4.56 right of pad 1 shows the 4.56 between the two pad centers.
    • Hover over a pad to see its size, position, mask and paste. The lines that made it light up. Click it to jump to the statement that defines it.
    • Drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom, double-click to fit. The preview follows the footprint your cursor is in.
    • 3D shows the part on a board. The model comes from the body, pins and lead form: lead gullwing ... bends gull-wing leads down to the span, lead ball puts balls under a substrate, lead endcap caps a chip. A model block changes the standoff, height, draft, colors and pin-1 mark, or adds boxes and cylinders. lead 4 gullwing 3.0 x 0.9 gives one pin its own lead and land. You can export the model as VRML (for KiCad) or STL.
    • Export to KiCad saves every footprint in the file as a KiCad 9 library (a zipped .pretty folder). Each footprint carries its own 3D model, so there's no model folder.
    • Libraries: a template can name the footprints it makes (name: "QFN-{n}-..."), and footprints from qfn, a table and end make one per table row. Library shows every footprint in the file. Put the cursor on a row to see that one. The libraries are at the bottom of the example picker.
    • Units and sizes: numbers are in mm unless the file or a footprint says units: mil, or a number has its own unit (50mil). Mark a size's sides when a drawing gives the height first: rect 1.6h x 1.1w.
    • Custom marks: define feature plus once (its lines, then end), then use it as pin1: plus or place it with feature plus at pad 2.
    • The models are stomp too. Every body, lead and mark is drawn by a feature of the standard library, whose files are at the bottom of the example picker. Edit one and every model that uses it follows. In your own file, a feature with a library feature's name replaces it (feature can_vent(D) … end), and body shape: NAME in a footprint's model block gives one footprint its own body. A feature chooses with sections, turned on by flags in the call: feature tick(long) at … includes its section long. See the Custom models example.
    • In the editor: Tab indents, Ctrl/⌘+/ toggles comments, Esc then Tab leaves the editor.

    Choices the draft leaves open

    Where the spec doesn't settle a meaning, the demo picks one:

    • Generated pads are sized across × along. For pins ..., W x L is the width across the row by the length along the lead, and each side's pads turn to face outward. That's how default pad rect 0.6 x 2.2 reads in the SOIC hand-solder variant.
    • The origin rule places floating layouts. A footprint placed only by relations (TO-252) has no fixed starting point, so it's solved from its first object and then centered on the body.
    • One coordinate frame. at (x, y) and graphics use the same coordinates. origin then moves the finished footprint's (0, 0).
    • IPC-style lands use IPC-7351B fillet goals with nominal part dimensions (no part tolerances), F = 0.05 mm, P = 0.025 mm, rounded corners at 25%. Ball lands are 80% of the ball. Mask expansion defaults to 0.05 mm per side.
    • Courtyards are rectangles. The automatic one is the smallest rectangle around the lands and body, plus the IPC clearance. A drawn one is courtyard: rect W x L.
    • Interpolation prints numbers in their shortest form. Lengths print in the file's units, and {e:.2} sets two decimals.
    • The body's height is the seated height (JEDEC A), from the board to the top of the part. The body sits on its standoff (A1), which defaults by lead form: 0.1 mm for gull-wings, 0.02 mm for leadless parts, three quarters of the ball for a BGA.
    • Small grammar extensions. omit and only take name ranges (only 1..5, used in the syntax zoo), and sizes can be written without spaces (0.6x1.5).

    Not in this demo

    step files aren't loaded. A footprint that names one shows the generated model instead, and its KiCad export points to the file rather than embedding a model. Castellated leads get a temporary land until the spec settles them (open question 17.7), though the model draws their plating. Export is for KiCad 9 and later only.

    Layers

    Copper
    pads, with drills and thermal vias
    Paste
    stencil openings, including windowpanes
    Mask
    solder-mask openings (off by default)
    Silk
    outline kept clear of copper, plus pin-1 and polarity marks
    Courtyard
    outline around the lands and body, plus IPC clearance
    Fab
    assembly outline and reference
    Constraints
    dimensions from placement statements
